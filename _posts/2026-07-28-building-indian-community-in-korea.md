When I moved to Korea in 2020, I knew almost no one here. Five years later, the
Indian community in Korea feels like family — and building that community has
become one of the most rewarding parts of my life outside engineering.

## IITAAK — from Public Relations to President

I joined the [IIT Alumni Association of Korea (IITAAK)](http://www.iitalumkorea.org/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}
soon after arriving, and served as **Vice President (Public Relations)** from
2021 before taking over as **President in 2023**. What started as a small circle
of IIT alumni has grown into an active community that meets throughout the
year — from our [Diwali camping trips]({% post_url 2022-10-19-iitaak-diwali-camping-and-fun %})
to networking dinners, hikes, and collaborations with businesses, academia and
the Indian Embassy in Seoul.

## India Day Seoul 2026

The highlight of this year was **India Day Seoul 2026** — the Embassy of
India's first large-scale outdoor festival, held on **May 16, 2026 at the
Neoreundeul Lawn Area, Yeouido Hangang Park**. Alongside the cultural
performances and food stalls, we hosted the
[**Fun Cinema & VFX Booth**](/india-day-seoul-2026/vfx/) — a playful
celebration of Indian film culture where visitors became the stars of their own
Bollywood-style movie moments, posing with iconic landmarks and show-stopping
visual effects.

Watching Korean families, students and the Indian diaspora laugh together in
front of a green screen was everything community work should feel like.

## Speaking at the India–Korea Forum on AI

In July 2026 I had the honour of speaking at the **India–Korea Forum on AI
2026** at Ewha Womans University, Seoul — a day-long forum under the theme
*"Fostering India–Korea AI Cooperation in a New Digital Era"*, bringing
together policymakers, academics, entrepreneurs and industry experts from both
countries. Sharing a stage dedicated to the two countries I call home, on the
technology I work on every day, is a memory I will keep for a long time.

And of course, every year there are the **IITAAK anniversary and year-end
celebrations** — evenings of food, culture and laughter that remind all of us
why this community matters.

## Beyond alumni — IIK and ISRK

This year I also took on two new roles: **Core Team Member of Indians in Korea
(IIK)**, one of the largest communities of the Indian diaspora here, and
**Executive Secretary of Indian Students and Researchers in Korea (ISRK)**,
supporting the students and researchers who are just beginning their journey in
Korea the way I once did.

If you are an Indian in Korea — a professional, a student, a researcher, or
just curious — come say hi at one of our events, or simply
[reach out](/#contact). The community is always open, and there is a place for
you in it.
